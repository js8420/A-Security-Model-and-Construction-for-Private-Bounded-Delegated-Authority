  salt elements per leaf                                  4
  random codewords per matrix                             2
  bare poseidon2, degree 7, zero registers             true

  Zero registers makes every S-box constraint degree 7; one register
  makes them degree 3. Both verify under the hiding PCS at the rate
  this crate now uses. At blowup 4, which it used until the sweep,
  degree 7 did not verify: the quotient a hiding commitment can carry
  is bounded by the rate. The register was a consequence of the rate
  and not of zero knowledge. The widths below are measured at one
  register, and the section near the end measures what zero costs.

NULLIFIER FROM A SECOND INVOCATION: THE PRICE OF DROPPING AN ASSUMPTION
==============================================================================
  depth / cover                           width    constraints
  ---------------------------------- ---------- --------------
  16 / 64, as built                        6596           6655
  16 / 64, second invocation              12612          12671
  16 / 30, second invocation               5982           6007
  16 / 128, second invocation             25092          25215

  The accountability half grows 6596 to 12612, and the composed circuit
  from 14292 to about 20308, which is +42%. What it buys is that the
  nullifier is a pseudorandom function of the key rather than a
  lane published beside it, so exculpability and bound privacy need
  no joint-lane assumption. We report the price; we do not pay it.

  two proofs share blinding, fresh seeds              false

  A column the same in every row is fixed by its value and the n
  random entries beside it. With more openings than that, the value
  is the unique solution of a linear system, and the attack finds it.
  Its failure at the deployed height is a check, not the argument;
  the argument is the height condition above.
